Fall Line Open the app

Find the fastest safe sledding hills in Westchester.

Fall Line reads LiDAR terrain and Westchester County GIS data, rides a simulated sled down every slope, and ranks the courses by speed and length — clear of roads, driveways, buildings and water.

Open Fall LineHow it works

Three taps to a ranked list

No drawing, no guessing. Point it at a hill and let the terrain do the talking.

1

Pick a square

Drag the map so the dashed square (½, 1 or 1½ miles) covers the hills you care about, or jump straight to a county park.

2

Press Find

Fall Line downloads the LiDAR terrain for that square, builds a hazard map from county planimetrics, and rides a sled from every steep spot.

3

Read the ranking

Ten distinct courses, scored by top speed × length. Tap one to zoom in over the county's 2-ft contours and see its elevation profile.

What every course has to pass

A course only survives if a simulated sled — gravity, sliding friction, air drag and a 0.3 g cornering limit — can ride it top to bottom and come to rest on its own.

Stays 125 ft from roadwaysMeasured to the edge of pavement, not the centerline. Adjustable.
No pitch over 30°Steep is the point; a drop-off is not. The cliff limit is a slider.
Clear of buildings by 40 ftDriveways and parking lots get a 30 ft buffer.
Lakes and streams off limitsIce at the bottom of a hill is not a runout.
Stops on its ownThe run ends where the sled comes to rest, so every course has a real runout.
Turns a sled can makeCornering is capped at 0.3 g, so a 30 mph run needs about 200 ft of radius.

Under the hood

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no account.

USGS 3DEP LiDARBare-earth elevation, fetched as image tiles and decoded to a terrain grid of roughly 3 m cells. 3DEP
Westchester County GIS2023 planimetrics (buildings, roadway pavement, driveways, parking, lakes, streams), open space, tax parcels and the county's 2-ft contours. giswww.westchestergov.com
Sled physicsGravity along the slope, friction coefficient 0.05, air drag for a rider on a plastic sled, and a lateral-acceleration cap that sets the turn radius from speed.
RankingScore = top speed (mph) × length (ft). Overlapping runs are collapsed so the ten courses shown are distinct lines.

The fine print

  • Fall Line is a screening tool built from bare-earth terrain. It does not see fences, stone walls, individual trees, or last week's plow pile. Walk the hill before anyone rides it.
  • Courses marked "parkland" sit on county-mapped open space. Public land does not permit clearing trees or shaping terrain; check with the parks department.
  • Courses not on mapped open space are probably private property. Turn on Tax parcels in the app and confirm the owner with the town assessor before doing anything.
  • The county's 2-ft contours date from 2004; the analyzed elevation is newer LiDAR. On regraded ground they can disagree by a foot or two.
